**The five rules** (strict = all five, soft = R1+R3+R4):
1. mark falling (below last time out) 2. below last winning mark 3. below career-best
mark 4. proven at today's trip (within 110 yards) 5. top-four last time out.
Scope: **handicaps only**, minimum 5 runners, live runners with a rating and history.

**The three daily-scan angles are NOT the same tests** (`backtest_tips_history.py`):
* Big Weight Drop = mark delta ≤ **−8lb** AND best Topspeed ≥ 60
* Value Qualifier = below last winning mark + ≥1 trip placing + LTO top-4 (**no career-max test**)
* Placed at Trip = **≥3** trip placings + LTO 2nd/3rd + TS ≥ 60

The results ledger stores all of this under the key `"Tips"`; the app displays it as
"Ben (Qas)" via `SYSTEM_DISPLAY`. The ledger's `sub_system` splits angles by drop size
(14 values, mostly one bet) — the app collapses them back to the three.

### ⚠️ The place leg is broken — levels are not usable, only ranking is

The all-runner control cannot be +8.7%, let alone +43% with an extra place. The model
pays the place leg as a **fixed fraction of the BSP win price**, which overpays every
longshot (a 62/1 shot "pays" a 13.4 place dividend for a place the market prices near 7).
Big Weight Drop is the longest-priced angle (avg 28.5), so it is the most flattered.

**Therefore:** the *ranking* (Big Weight Drop > Value Qualifier ≈ Placed at Trip; at SP
all three lose, less than the field) survives; **every BSP ROI level is inflated** by an
unknown amount until the place leg is settled at a real place price.

### Extra places — cannot be answered yet

Re-settling the same picks with +1 / +2 places:

| picks | std | +1 place | +2 places |
|---|---|---|---|
| every runner (control) | +8.7% | +43.1% | +91.0% |
| Big Weight Drop | +8.2% | +34.6% | +64.9% |
| Value Qualifier | −1.6% | +17.0% | +36.1% |
| Placed at Trip | −3.0% | +14.3% | +31.9% |

Those extra-place gains are an artefact of the same broken place leg (+43% for backing
the whole field). **The direction is real** — one extra place lifts the placed share of
Big Weight Drop picks from 31.5% to 43.4% — but the size must be measured against real
place prices (the ledger already records `bf_place_odds` / `early_place_odds`, and the
cloud snapshots now capture bookmaker EW terms + Betfair place back/lay).

## 8. Extra places — availability, and how to bet it

**Now captured on every logged pick** (`scripts/selection_price_log.py`): `field_size`,
`places_std` (16+ runners → 4, 8+ → 3, else 2), `places_offered` (best in the market), and
an `extra_place` flag. Report section 6 splits ROI by the flag once results land.
Verified on today's pick: Bread And Butter, 16:38 Plumpton — 8 runners, standard 3,
offered 3 → `extra_place 0`.

**How often it is there** (2026-09-19 snapshot, 50 races / 575 runners):

* races with an extra place on offer: **29 of 50 (58%)**
* runners covered: **368 of 575 (64%)**
* (standard, offered) counts: `(3,3):17 (3,4):17 (4,4):2 (4,5):4 (4,6):2 (4,7):1 (2,2):2 (2,3):3 (3,5):2`

**Do NOT log only the extra-place races.** Dropping the rest removes the control group and
then nothing can distinguish "the promotion paid" from "the picks paid" — the same mistake
that made today's other figures unusable. Log everything, carry the flag, compare the two
halves. Betting is where the filter belongs: *only stake when the offer is live at the book
you use* (and mind the stake caps and the shorter prices these offers carry).

**It cannot be back-tested.** The historical Racing Post frame holds only standard
`places_paid`; extra-place offers were never recorded. This must be measured forward — which
makes `selection_prices.csv` and the cloud snapshots the only source of truth for it.

Next refinement: availability is currently race-level (best offer in the market). For staking
you need it **per bookmaker** plus the cap — `BookOdds.EWPlaces` already carries it per book,
so that is a join, not new capture.

**Reading:**

* Caveats that stay attached: 85 and 55 picks in the two long bands, 37 and 42 at BSP, and one

### Which base holds the high odds? (added after §10)

Same picks, three bases, prices capped at 500 (the `bf_odds` 1000 excluded):

| band | n | avg early | avg SP | avg BSP | early > SP | early > BSP | **BSP > early** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | 29 | 2.31 | 2.14 | 2.56 | 66% | 7% | **93%** |
| 3–6 | 107 | 4.59 | 4.38 | 5.31 | 58% | 3% | **97%** |
| 6–12 | 114 | 8.41 | 8.07 | 11.26 | 55% | 3% | **97%** |
| 12–25 | 85 | 16.74 | 16.27 | 20.77 | 54% | 11% | **89%** |
| 25+ | 55 | 65.85 | 60.16 | **137.30** | 58% | 0% | **100%** |

* **Early beats SP** on 54–66% of picks and on the average in every band — so yes, the early price
  *is* the higher odds **against SP**.
* **BSP beats the early price** on 89–100% of picks, and on the outsiders it is not close
  (avg 137.3 vs 65.9 on 25/1+). So the *highest* prices for these picks are on the exchange,
  not in the morning books.
* Selection edge (strike minus implied) is **positive at early and at SP in every band**
  (+1.9 to +7.1pp), so the live profit is not merely price shopping; at BSP only 12/1+ is positive,
  and the short-band BSP samples are tiny (n=11, 48, 72 — do not lean on them).

Operational reading: for the outsiders, **check BSP against your early price per pick** rather
than assuming the morning book is the big price. The week's log is built to answer exactly that.

### Steam or drift?  It depends which market you measure

Movement from the recorded early price on the same picks:

| basis | n | median move | shortened | drifted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **to SP** (industry starting price) | 356 | **−6.7%** | **57.0%** | 25.0% |
| **to BSP** (Betfair starting price) | 183 | **+18.3%** | 9.3% | **88.5%** |

By band, the share that SHORTENED:

| band | to SP | to BSP |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | 66% (median −5.5%) | 18% (median +6.7%) |
| 3–6 | 61% (−6.9%) | 6% (+12.3%) |
| 6–12 | 57% (−5.9%) | 4% (+16.7%) |
| 12–25 | 54% (−9.5%) | 20% (+29.0%) |
| 25+ | 58% (**−14.7%**) | **0% (+97.5%)** |

**So the picks DO steam — into the industry SP** (57% shorten, median −6.7%, and the longer the
price the harder they shorten). **But they drift into Betfair's BSP**: 88.5% lengthen, median
+18.3%, and on the 25/1+ picks **none** shortened and the median move is **+97.5%** — BSP ends up
roughly double the morning bookmaker price.

Reason: SP is an average of bookmaker prices compressing through the day, while BSP is an
exchange auction at the off, and for outsiders the exchange settles far longer.

**What it is worth** (win-only, 2% commission on exchange wins):

| price taken | all picks with both prices (n=183) | early ≥ 12/1 (n=67) |
|---|---|---|
| early bookmaker price | +7.3% | +59.7% |
| **Betfair BSP** | **+37.4%** | **+107.9%** |
| best of the two per pick | +37.4% | +107.9% |

BSP was the better price on **89%** of picks (87% of the longshots). So on this evidence the
exchange, not the morning book, is where these picks should be taken — **and it re-opens the
forward book anomaly** (taken 14.1 vs BSP 16.1 yet better P&L), which now looks like an
understated BSP side rather than a real early-price advantage.

Caveats: n=183 / 67; BSP liquidity on 25/1+ outsiders is thin, so those 137.0-odd prices are
small-stake prices, and part of the drift is microstructure rather than opinion.

### Historical test of steam/drift — and it can only be done on the exchange

`PRODB.BFSP` holds three real exchange time points for **698,993 runners, 2020-12-31 →
2026-09-19**: `MorningWAP` (early), `PPWAP` (pre-off) and `BSP_TRUE` (at the off). That is enough
to test early-vs-off on real Betfair prices over 5.75 years. It is **not** enough to test the
*bookmaker* early price — that history starts 2026-09-15.

Column validated first: the per-race `sum(1/BSP)` has a **median of 1.003** over 8,743 races, i.e.
the implied probabilities sum to ~1.00 as a fair win market should, so `BSP_TRUE` is sound.

Win-only ROI at each time point (2% commission on wins), 645,481 runners:

| picks | n | MorningWAP (early) | PPWAP (pre-off) | BSP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| every runner (control) | 643,739 | −3.4% | **−10.1%** | +2.4% |
| **Big Weight Drop** | 44,156 | **+6.1%** | −5.7% | **+6.0%** |
| Value Qualifier | 74,106 | −5.7% | −11.6% | −2.1% |
| Placed at Trip | 7,851 | −4.5% | −12.3% | −3.0% |

* **The pre-off window is the worst price for everything** — the control loses 10.1% there against
  3.4% in the morning. Betting in the last few minutes is the single most expensive habit on this
  evidence.
* **Big Weight Drop pays the same early and at BSP** (+6.1% / +6.0%) and loses only pre-off. So on
  the exchange the early-vs-BSP choice does not decide that rule; it sits ~7pp above the control at
  BSP, which corroborates the +8.2% EW figure in §2.
* The control's +2.4% at BSP is a fat-tail fluctuation, not a free lunch (SE ≈ 1.2pp on 643k bets).

Movement, morning → BSP (median, share that shortened):

| picks | Morning → BSP | shortened |
|---|---|---|
| every runner (control) | +11.3% | 39% |
| Big Weight Drop | +5.9% | 44% |
| Value Qualifier | +5.9% | 42% |
| Placed at Trip | +3.6% | 45% |

Control by odds band — this is the general rule behind the live finding:

| BSP band | n | median move | shortened |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2–5 | 94,486 | **−8.8%** | **66%** |
| 5–10 | 147,984 | −1.9% | 53% |
| 10–20 | 146,640 | +10.9% | 38% |
| 20+ | 235,721 | **+56.9%** | **18%** |

**Short prices steam, long prices drift** — over 5.75 years and 640k runners. Our picks shorten a
little more often than the field (44% vs 39%), which is the mild steamer tendency, but the "we can
predict drifters/steamers" claim is really a *price-band* effect, not a selection one.
