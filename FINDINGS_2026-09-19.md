## 2. The two feeds measure different things

Matched runner-by-runner, same runs:

| | matched | SData | RaceIQ v2 | bias | r | same horse tops the race |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| speed | 50,708 | 33.09 mph | 37.80 mph | **+4.72** | 0.80 | **33.6%** |
| stride | 21,782 | 6.97 m | 7.24 m | +0.26 | 0.38 | **50.3%** |

SData's `MPH_Finish` is a **finishing** speed; RaceIQ's `TopSpeedMph` is a
**peak**. A horse that led and faded scores well on one, badly on the other —
which is why, in the same races, the two feeds name a different "best horse" two
times in three. SData's stride is in **feet** (22.88 ft = 6.97 m); RaceIQ's is
metres, and RaceIQ's own `StrideFt` confirms the conversion.

## 3. RaceIQ publishes a different metric set for jumps — a real bug

Flat: `0-20MPH`, `Stride Length`, `FSP`, `Top Speed`.
Jumps: `Jump Index` (/10), `LGJ` (lengths gained jumping), `FSP`, `Top Speed`,
`Speed Lost` (**negative**), `Entry Speed` — and **no stride, no 0-20MPH at all**.

`raceiq_parse.py` knew only the flat four and skipped the rest silently, so every
jump meeting looked like "missing stride data". It wasn't missing, we weren't
reading it. **Fixed today** — four columns, an idempotent migration, and the raw
page archive (see §10).

## 6. Why — three causes, two of them fixable

* **The column** (§4) — the app ranks on peak speed, not finishing speed.
* **Stale priors** — in the 2026 window **39.7%** of v2 picks rest on a prior
  older than 90 days (SData: 14.9%), because v2 covers ~36% of fields and jump
  meetings publish no stride. The backfill now running fixes this half.
* **Definition** — `TopSpeed × FspPct/100` correlates 0.91 with SData's reading
  yet still cannot reproduce the audited edge as a *rule* (+0.81% net in 2025,
  −7.43% in 2026). RaceIQ publishes **no absolute finishing-speed column**;
  `FspPct` is relative to the horse's own average (mean 101).

**Ruled out:** coverage breadth (SData +11.2% vs v2 −3.8% on *shared* races
only) and the lag mechanics (prior-to-prior disagreement 4.60 mph vs same-run
4.59 mph — the lag adds no error of its own).

## 9. TPD access — what is licensed, what is not

The websocket is a **documented, licensed integration**: a one-time access key is
minted server-side, bound to *your* API key + streaming vendor key + the race id
+ the client's IP. There is no stable way to "just scrape" it.
`tpd.zone` membership is a **live in-play** licence key for BetMover (TPD's own,
free), Bet Angel, Bf Bot Manager or Gruss Betting Assistant — **no historical or
replay access**. Post-race and historical data is the corporate **Live &
Post-Race data API** (JSON RPC; examples in their public `gmaxfeed` repo).
Proform is a licensed TPD reseller, which is why SData and RaceIQ rhyme.

**Rule: never settle or evaluate on BSP alone.** `auto_daily_pipeline.py:103`
already builds the morning → off-time history in `BookOdds`, so the machinery
exists — but bookmaker-level early history is **five days deep**, which is not
enough to evaluate anything on. For history the only early reference is
`BFSP.PPWAP` / `MorningWAP`.

Early prices cannot be reconstructed later — Betfair does not hand back a past
morning's bookmaker prices. So: keep logging them daily;
`BookOdds` = the price taken, `BFSP.PPWAP` = historical fallback, `BSP` = the
comparison, never the answer.
